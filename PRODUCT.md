# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who write on a Mac and want to replace keyboard-heavy drafting with fast voice dictation. They use VoiceInk while working in other applications, often through global shortcuts, and expect it to stay out of the way while preserving privacy and control.

## Product Purpose

VoiceInk turns speech into usable text at the current cursor with low friction. It combines local and cloud transcription options, context-aware formatting, reusable modes, AI enhancement, and a personal dictionary. Success means users can dictate accurately across their existing macOS workflow without moving text between apps or surrendering control of sensitive content.

## Brand Personality

Native, focused, trustworthy. VoiceInk should feel like a carefully made macOS utility: quick in daily use, calm in presentation, and explicit about permissions or behavior that affects other applications.

## Anti-references

- Web-dashboard styling that ignores familiar macOS controls and interaction conventions.
- Decorative surfaces, excessive animation, or promotional copy inside task-focused settings.
- Hidden automation that changes user text without an understandable setting or safe failure behavior.
- Dense technical language where a short, concrete explanation is sufficient.

## Design Principles

1. Keep dictation in the user’s existing workflow instead of creating a separate writing environment.
2. Prefer native macOS affordances and predictable settings over novel controls.
3. Make privacy-sensitive automation local, bounded, and easy to disable.
4. Preserve user intent and existing configuration; uncertain automation should safely do nothing.
5. Keep frequent actions immediate while placing advanced detail behind progressive disclosure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use standard macOS controls, semantic labels, keyboard navigation, system appearance, Dynamic Type-compatible text styles, and adequate contrast. Respect reduced-motion settings and never rely on color alone for status. Permission-dependent features must degrade safely when Accessibility or other system access is unavailable.
